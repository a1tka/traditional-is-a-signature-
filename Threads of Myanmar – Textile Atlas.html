Shared foundations

Longyi / paso: a wrap-around garment widely worn across Myanmar. Men’s versions are commonly called paso, women’s htamein. Upper garments, jackets and shawls vary by community and occasion, from everyday wear to ceremonial dress. Stripes, checks, borders, embroidery and woven motifs can signal local style and craft knowledge.

How to read a traditional outfit

1. SilhouetteWrap, skirt, tunic, trousers, jacket, shawl or head covering.
2. TextileHandwoven cotton or silk, stripes, checks, borders, embroidery.
3. ColourMay be locally meaningful, but don’t assume a universal code.
4. AdornmentJewellery, headwear, belts and accessories can carry family or ceremonial significance.
5. OccasionDaily wear, weddings, festivals, worship and stage costumes look different.

Beyond the eight

Many more communities have their own textile and adornment traditions, including Lisu, Naga, Pa-O, Ta’ang, Wa, Kokang, Intha, Danu and Akha. These are examples, not a complete list. Communities are grouped differently in official, academic and local sources; consult community-led references for precise identification.

Presenting respectfully

  • Name the community when known
  • Avoid calling one outfit universal
  • Ask permission before photographing people
  • Credit weavers and cultural groups
  • Don’t treat sacred or ceremonial items as costumes
  • Tradition keeps changing: it is living heritage
